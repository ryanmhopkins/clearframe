A repeatable pre-delivery workflow

  1. Start with copies. Keep your originals and their metadata in your archive. Choose the exported photos you actually plan to share.
  2. Inspect a representative file. Scan a photo and review GPS, dates, author information, captions, and editing metadata. Not every file contains every field.
  3. Choose what to remove. Keep copyright information you want attached. For JPEG, PNG, WebP, and GIF, choices remove whole blocks; removing EXIF also removes its other tags, potentially including orientation.
  4. Apply deliberately. Free lets you add multiple files and review each one. Pro adds folder selection and saved presets you can apply across a batch. Check the choices on files from different cameras or editors.
  5. Export and verify. Download individual copies or a ZIP. Re-scan a cleaned copy, open it in your normal photo viewer, and check its appearance before delivery.

Keep the photograph in its original format

ClearFrame does not resize or recompress the image. Removing orientation or color metadata can still affect how a viewer displays it. Color profiles and detected credentials are kept by default; review those choices rather than removing everything automatically.

RAW is a selective cleanup

Supported RAW files keep maker notes, calibration, previews, and required rendering data. Those retained areas may contain identifying information. CR3, RAF, RW2, PEF, and unlisted RAW formats are not supported. A browser may clean a supported file even when it cannot show a preview.

Practical limits before a large delivery

The per-file limit is 256 MB. Browser memory can impose a lower practical batch size: split large jobs into smaller batches. Keep the tab open until the download finishes. The free limit counts each photo in a ZIP and resets at your device’s local midnight.

What cleaning cannot do

Cleaning does not remove visible faces, house numbers, watermarks, or information hidden in pixels. Filenames and filesystem dates are separate. ClearFrame does not promise forensic anonymity or detection of every proprietary field. Review what you share, including the photograph itself.
