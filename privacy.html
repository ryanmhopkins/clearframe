Privacy policy

This page describes what the ClearFrame website does with information. It is written in plain language. It is not a lawyer-drafted policy.

Who this policy covers

This policy is about ClearFrame, this website. No company name or postal address is published here. Questions go to the contact page.

Photos you choose

Scanning and cleaning happen in your browser. The photo files you select are not uploaded to this site or to any other server by this page. Cleaned copies and ZIP downloads are created in the browser and saved by your browser.

Free use does not ask for your name or email, does not set a sign-in cookie, and does not take payment details. Photos and their metadata are not sent anywhere in the free cleaner or in ClearFrame Pro.

What this site downloads

Loading ClearFrame downloads the page, its scripts, its styles, and its fonts from this website. HEIC, HEIF, AVIF, and TIFF photos also cause the browser to download a photo engine of about 25 MB from this same website the first time you need it. ClearFrame Pro does the same for RAW photos. That file is the engine, not your photo. JPEG, PNG, WebP, and GIF cleaning does not download that engine.

Scripts, fonts, and the photo engine are part of this site. The page does not load them from a third-party font host, a public script CDN, or another company’s servers.

How your privacy works explains how to confirm this in your browser’s network tab.

What the host can see

Opening this website connects to the host that serves it at getclearframe.app. Like other websites, that connection can include your IP address, the time, your browser’s user agent, and the address of the page you requested. Your photos are not part of those requests.

This project’s files do not configure or read those host logs, and this page cannot turn them off. How long the host keeps them is the host’s setting, not something stated here because this repository does not control it.

ClearFrame Pro

Pro is optional. Signing in sends the email address you type to this website. The site asks the payment provider whether that email has a paid ClearFrame Pro purchase, then emails a sign-in link if it does. The page shows the same confirmation either way. The link expires after 15 minutes.

Opening the link stores one sign-in cookie for 30 days. The cookie holds a signed copy of that email and the time the purchase was last checked. Page scripts cannot read it. Purchase status is checked when you sign in and again at most once a day. If the payment provider no longer reports a paid purchase, Pro access stops at the next check. Signing out deletes the cookie.

The site does not receive your photos or photo metadata. The anonymous counts below do not include photos, file names, or metadata. Presets you save in Pro stay in your browser. Managing a receipt opens the payment provider’s own page. Card numbers are not entered on this website.

Cookies, analytics, and ads

The free cleaner’s scripts do not set cookies. The only cookie this site sets is the Pro sign-in cookie described above, and only after a successful sign-in. There is no advertising on these pages.

Analytics data is processed in the United States. The pages send anonymous counts to PostHog Cloud (US / Virginia). The analytics script is a file on this website. The counts go directly to https://us.i.posthog.com. They are not sent through getclearframe.app, and they are not sent with the Pro sign-in cookie. Choosing, scanning, or cleaning a photo does not upload the photo. A count sent after a scan or a clean does not contain the photo, its file name, or its metadata.

What is counted is the page path, with no query string and no hash, the referring site’s domain, and the campaign tags utm_source, utm_medium, and utm_campaign, plus anonymous counts of actions such as a clean finishing. Those counts use coarse buckets only, such as 2-5 files or JPEG, plus a click that starts checkout. What is never collected is file names, photo contents, EXIF, GPS, or camera values, exact sizes or dimensions, anything you type, names, email addresses, account IDs, session recordings, and heatmaps.

Analytics does not set a cookie. While analytics is on, it stores nothing except brief test writes that the library deletes right away. If you turn analytics off, this browser stores only that choice in local storage, so the choice remains after a reload. Turning it back on removes that stored choice. A browser that sends Do Not Track, or a Global Privacy Control signal, also stops these counts. The cleaner still works when analytics is off or when us.i.posthog.com is blocked.

PostHog can estimate one day’s visitors with a one-way hash of the IP address, browser details, and a salt that is rotated daily, and it can discard the IP address instead of storing it on the event. Those are project settings: Cookieless server hash mode, and Discard client IP data. This page does not say those settings are already on. Until they are, the analytics request still reaches PostHog in the United States, and the IP address is part of that connection.

Analytics events are kept for up to 1 year on the PostHog Free plan. Payments are handled separately by Polar. Email, name, and card details from a purchase are not sent to analytics. This description is not a claim of legal compliance.

Questions about these counts: support@getclearframe.app.

Children

ClearFrame is a general photo tool. It is not directed at children, and it does not offer accounts or a place to send personal details. It also does not receive the photos you open.

Changes
